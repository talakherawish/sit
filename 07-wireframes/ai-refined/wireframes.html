Flow 1 · Renter app

Check before you go

Is there space, is it open, does the Wi-Fi work? A renter answers this from home, before deciding to come in.

0taps to the answer
Tap the Wi-Fi tile+1 · optional
Close, tap the seat count+1 · optional
  1. 1

    The live seat count is the largest thing on the screen because it answers the first question: is there space?Visual hierarchy

  2. 2

    Hours, cafeteria and Wi-Fi as three tiles with one short value each, instead of a long status list.Miller's Law · chunking

  3. 3

    Wi-Fi shows the measured speed (↓ 94 Mbps), not "Normal", so a renter can tell if a video call will work.Useful information over vague status

  4. 4

    Only the latest notice is shown, with a count. The rest open in a sheet.Avoid too much information

  5. 5

    Book a room is the only filled button on the first screen. It opens the Rooms tab, for people who know when but not where.Von Restorff · make the main action obvious

  6. 6

    Everything above the fold answers "should I come in?". The floor plan and room tools start below it, as their own sections.Visual hierarchy · sectioning

  7. 7

    The floor plan matches the real building. The key fits on one line and every state has a word, not only a colour.Familiar mental model · accessibility

  8. 8

    "What are you here to do?" offers four choices, then lists only the rooms that suit it.Hick's Law

  9. 9

    Speed tests through the day explain the earlier "back to full speed" notice.Visibility of system status

  10. 10

    The sheet says why there is nothing to book here, so nobody looks for a missing button.Prevent errors · clear expectations

Flow 2 · Renter app · core booking

Book from the map

Tala needs Big Room 2 for two hours this morning. She finds it on the plan, picks a time on its day, says what it's for and is done.

6taps from Home to booked
Tap Big Room 2+1
Tap 10:00, then 11:30+2
Book 10:00–12:00+1
Pick a reason, Confirm+2
  1. 1

    The map and the Rooms tab open the same room sheet, so booking works the same wherever you start.Consistency · Jakob's Law (bottom sheets)

  2. 2

    Six days in a strip. Closed days (Friday) are faded and can't be picked.Error prevention

  3. 3

    The room's whole opening day, with booked stretches labelled by time, instead of a list of free slots that hides when it's taken. Half-hour rows are 44 px tall in the real UI.Visibility of system status · Fitts's Law

  4. 4

    The hint line says what the next tap will do and changes after each tap. If a booking is in the way, it says so and starts again from the tapped time.Feedback after every action

  5. 5

    The main button sits at the bottom, full width, under the thumb. It reads "Pick a time" and stays disabled until a time is chosen, then names the booking.Fitts's Law · Von Restorff · prevent errors

  6. 6

    Room, day and time are already chosen, so the review page only asks what it's for: one tap on a chip instead of the full form again.Hick's Law · fewer steps

  7. 7

    The release rule is explained where the person commits, not in a help page.Set expectations · prevent no-shows

  8. 8

    A clear ending: a confirmation page with every detail, plus "Booked · Undo" for a few seconds in case of a mistake.Peak-End Rule · let users recover

Flow 3 · Renter app

Book from the Rooms tab

For people who know when, not where. "Book a room" on Home opens this tab.

5taps from Home to booked
Tap 10:00 in Focus 2+1
Book, reason, Confirm+3
R-06 → R-08Same review and confirmation as Flow 2.

Or switch to List

  1. 1

    Two views of the same rooms: a day grid to compare times, or a plain list to compare rooms. The switch sits at the top.User control · flexibility

  2. 2

    Every room side by side for one day, like a calendar app: booked blocks, your own bookings outlined. Free time is the empty space, so it's quick to spot.Jakob's Law · visibility of status

  3. 3

    The tapped time arrives already picked (one hour), so the sheet only needs Book.Clicks to treasure

  4. 4

    The list groups rooms by size and says when each is free next. Walk-in seating and amenities sit in the same list, so nothing needs a separate screen.Miller's Law · chunking

Flow 4 · Renter app

Book several days

Same room, same time, more than one day: one switch in the room sheet.

8taps from Home
Book 2 days+1
  1. 1

    Several days from a room you already like: one switch in the same sheet, instead of a separate form several screens deep.Clicks to treasure

  2. 2

    Dots mark days that are free at the chosen time. Faded days are closed or taken and can't be picked.Prevent errors · labels plus marks

  3. 3

    The same review page as a single booking, with one chip per day. Days that can't be booked are named before confirming and skipped.Consistency · error prevention over error messages

  4. 4

    For later bookings the person picks when to be reminded. The release rule sits right under it.User control · set expectations

Flow 5 · Renter app · after booking

Keep or cancel a booking

A reminder arrives a few hours before. Confirming keeps the room; no answer releases it for someone else. This is how Sit cuts no-shows.

1tap to confirm or cancel
Or open My bookings+1
Tap Cancel+1
Cancel booking+1
  1. 1

    The reminder carries both answers. Confirming or cancelling is one tap, and cancelling still gets an Undo.Clicks to treasure

  2. 2

    The release rules, in plain words, at the moment the person decides.Set expectations · prevent no-shows

  3. 3

    Sort by soonest, oldest, latest date or recently booked.User control and freedom

  4. 4

    Actions that aren't possible stay visible but disabled, with the reason underneath, so nobody wonders where the button went.Prevent errors · consistency

  5. 5

    Status is written out next to a mark (Awaiting confirmation, Confirmed, Used). Colour is never the only signal.Accessibility

  6. 6

    Cancelling asks first and says what happens next. For a repeating booking it asks "only this one or the whole series".Error prevention

  7. 7

    Undo on the message after cancelling. A wrong tap costs nothing.Let users recover from mistakes

R-17 My reportsSame as frame 5, with the status and staff reply.
  1. 1

    Where is filled in already: public seating by default, or the room you came from when you tap "Report a problem with this room" on its details page.Reduce typing · context

  2. 2

    Several issue types at once, because real problems come together. One report instead of two.Match user needs

  3. 3

    Send stays disabled until at least one issue is picked. The note and photo are optional.Prevent errors · keep it short

  4. 4

    Sending lands on My reports, with the new report marked "Sent" and a short confirmation.Feedback after every action

  5. 5

    Reception sees a count on Reports in the sidebar and a "new reports" card on Today, so a report never waits unseen.Visibility of system status

  6. 6

    One row per report with status and reply side by side. Save stays disabled until something changes.Prevent errors · fewer clicks

  7. 7

    A banner drops in the moment reception saves, over whatever screen Tala has open. Tapping it opens the report.Feedback after every action · visibility of system status

  8. 8

    The bell keeps a dot until the alerts are opened, so a missed banner isn't lost.Recognition over recall

  9. 9

    Tala sees each step (Sent → Seen → In progress → Fixed) and the staff reply under her report.Visibility of system status · close the loop

  10. 10

    Notifications groups alerts as New and Earlier, each with an icon and the one action it needs.Visual hierarchy

Flow 7 · Reception · desktop

Run the front desk

Rana, at reception, starts the day by posting the status and keeping the seat count right, then checks people in as they arrive. Same look and words as the renter app.

2clicks to check someone in
Click Fatima's booking, or Check-in+1
  1. 1

    If today's status isn't posted, a banner says so with a link to fix it. It clears once staff post a notice marked as the daily status.Make important actions obvious · feedback

  2. 2

    Large − and + buttons for the seat count, and a reminder that renters see this exact number. Reset asks first and is logged.Fitts's Law · visibility of system status

  3. 3

    Every room's day on one timeline with a line for now. Booking state is shown by fill and outline, with a key.Visibility of status · labels, not colour alone

  4. 4

    Click a booking on the timeline to check its renter in, without searching.Fewer clicks for the most common desk task

  5. 5

    Today's bookings sort by time, room, renter or status, because the desk gets different questions.User control

  6. 6

    A new walk-in uses the same sign-up form as the app: name, phone, email.Consistency

  7. 7

    A booking only counts from 30 minutes before it starts. Someone arriving earlier is a walk-in until then, and the button says so.Prevent errors · match the real process

  8. 8

    The main button names exactly what will happen ("Check in · Focus Room 2"). When public seating is full, the walk-in button says so and is disabled.Feedback · prevent errors

Edge states · Renter app

When something goes wrong

Each screen says what happened and offers a way forward.

  1. 1

    The clash is explained in one sentence, followed by other free times in the same room and other rooms free at the same time.Help users recover from errors

  2. 2

    A room that is down says why, and the only button leads to another room.Visibility of status · a way forward

Reference

Where each principle shows up

F2·5 means Flow 2, note 5.

PrincipleWhat it saysWhere
Jakob's LawPeople expect it to work like apps they knowTab bar, bottom sheets, segmented controls, calendar-style day view (F2·1, F3·2)
Fitts's LawImportant targets are big and easy to reachFull-width bottom buttons (F2·5), 44 px timeline rows (F2·3), − / + seat buttons (F7·2)
Hick's LawMore choices, slower decisionsFour activity chips (F1·8), review asks only the reason (F2·6)
Miller's LawShow information in small groupsThree status tiles (F1·2), rooms grouped by size (F3·4)
Von RestorffThe item that looks different gets noticedOne filled button per screen (F1·5, F2·5)
Peak-End RulePeople remember the peak and the endingConfirmation page with Undo (F2·8)
Clicks to treasureFewest steps to the main valueStatus on open (F1), time arrives picked (F3·3), more days in the same sheet (F4·1), one-tap confirm (F5·1)
Visibility of system statusAlways show what is going onLive seat count (F1·1), measured Wi-Fi (F1·3), alert when a report changes (F6·7), report status and reply (F6·9)
Prevent errors, allow recoveryStop mistakes early; make them cheap to undoDisabled until ready (F2·5, F6·3), skipped days named first (F4·3), Undo (F5·7), Slot just taken (E·1)
AccessibilityWorks for everyone, in contextWords next to every colour state (F1·7, F5·5), Arabic right-to-left, 44 px targets

Sit wireframes

Low-fidelity blueprints of Sit's core flows, traced from the clickable prototype. They show structure, navigation and the order of steps. Colour, photos and branding are left out on purpose, so the review stays on one question: does the structure and the flow work?

  • Renter app · phone, 390 × 844
  • Reception dashboard · desktop
  • 6 flows · 5 edge states · 25 frames
  • Screen IDs match the prototype spec (R-01…R-21, S-01…S-10)

Flow 1 · Renter app · no account needed

Check before you go

"Is there space, is it open, and does the Wi-Fi work?" A renter answers this from bed, before deciding to come in.

0taps to the answer
Tap the Wi-Fi tile+1 tap · optionalDetail is one tap away, never in the way
Close, tap the seat count+1 tap · optional
  1. 1

    Guests see a language switch and Log in, nothing else. Browsing never asks for an account; we ask only when the person tries to book or report.Reduce friction · clicks to treasure

  2. 2

    The live seat count is the largest thing on the screen because it answers the first question: is there space?Visual hierarchy

  3. 3

    Hours, cafeteria and Wi-Fi as three tiles with one short value each, instead of a long status list.Miller's Law · chunking

  4. 4

    Wi-Fi shows the measured speed (↓ 94 Mbps), not "Normal". A renter can tell if a video call will work.Useful information over vague status

  5. 5

    Only the latest notice is shown, with a count. The rest open in a sheet.Avoid too much information

  6. 6

    Book a room closes the first screen as the only filled button, for people who know when but not where.Von Restorff · make the main action obvious

  7. 7

    Everything above the fold answers "should I come in?". The floor plan and room tools start below it, as separate sections.Visual hierarchy · sectioning

  8. 8

    The floor plan matches the real building. The key fits on one line and every state has a word, not only a colour.Familiar mental model · accessibility

  9. 9

    "What are you here to do?" offers four choices and then lists only the rooms that suit it.Hick's Law

  10. 10

    Speed tests through the day explain the earlier "back to full speed" notice.Visibility of system status

  11. 11

    The sheet says why there is nothing to book here, so nobody looks for a missing button.Prevent errors · clear expectations

Flow 2 · Renter app · core booking

Book a room from the map

Tala needs Big Room 2 for two hours this morning. She sees the room on the plan, picks a time on its day, says what it's for and is done.

6taps from Home to booked
Tap Big Room 2+1 tap
Tap 10:00, then 11:30+2 tapsStart, then stretch
Book 10:00–12:00+1 tapGuests meet the log-in sheet here (Edge states)
Pick a reason, Confirm+2 tapsConfirm stays disabled until a reason is picked
  1. 1

    The map and the Rooms tab open the same room sheet, so booking works the same wherever you start.Consistency · Jakob's Law (bottom sheets)

  2. 2

    Six days in a strip. Closed days (Friday) are faded and can't be picked.Error prevention

  3. 3

    The room's whole opening day, with booked stretches labelled by time, instead of a list of free slots that hides when it's taken. Half-hour rows are 44 px tall in the real UI.Visibility of system status · Fitts's Law

  4. 4

    The hint line says what the next tap will do and changes after each tap. If a booking is in the way, it says so and starts again from the tapped time.Feedback after every action

  5. 5

    The main button sits at the bottom, full width, under the thumb. It reads "Pick a time" and stays disabled until a time is chosen, then names the booking.Fitts's Law · Von Restorff · prevent errors

  6. 6

    Room, day and time are already chosen, so the review page only asks what it's for: one tap on a chip instead of the full form again.Hick's Law · fewer steps

  7. 7

    The release rule is explained where the person commits, not in a help page.Set expectations · prevent no-shows

  8. 8

    A clear ending: a confirmation page with every detail, plus "Booked · Undo" for a few seconds in case of a mistake.Peak-End Rule · let users recover

Regular renters book patterns ("every Thursday this month"), not single days. Two doors lead to the same review page.

Route B · I already know the room

Book 2 days+1 tap

  1. 1

    The patterns people actually book, side by side: One day, Weekly, Monthly (e.g. the 1st of each month) and Pick days (any days, any week).Understand user needs · familiar segmented control

  2. 2

    The page writes out the real dates it will book and leaves out closed days, so nobody has to work it out.Reduce cognitive load · visibility of status

  3. 3

    Results update while days, time or size change. There is no Search button. Each row says on how many of the days it's free and books from the row.Feedback · fewer clicks

  4. 4

    Days that can't be booked are named before confirming, not discovered after.Error prevention over error messages

  5. 5

    For later bookings, the person picks when to be reminded. The release rule sits right under it.User control · set expectations

  6. 6

    Several days from a room you already like: one switch in the same sheet, instead of a form several screens deep.Clicks to treasure

  7. 7

    Dots mark days that are free at the chosen time. Faded days are closed or taken and can't be picked.Prevent errors · labels plus marks

Flow 4 · Renter app · after booking

Keep or cancel a booking

A reminder arrives a few hours before. Confirming keeps the room; silence releases it for someone else. This is how Sit cuts no-shows.

1tap to confirm or cancel
Or open My bookings+1 tapAlso the third tab
Tap Cancel+1 tap
Cancel booking+1 tap
  1. 1

    The reminder carries both answers. Confirming or cancelling is one tap, and cancelling still gets an Undo.Clicks to treasure

  2. 2

    The release rules, in plain words, at the moment the person decides.Set expectations · prevent no-shows

  3. 3

    Status is written out next to a mark (Awaiting confirmation, Confirmed, Used). Colour is never the only signal.Accessibility

  4. 4

    Actions that aren't possible stay visible but disabled, with the reason underneath, so nobody wonders where the button went.Prevent errors · consistency

  5. 5

    Sort by soonest, oldest, latest date or recently booked. The newest booking is easy to find.User control and freedom

  6. 6

    Cancelling asks first and says what happens next. For a repeating booking it asks "only this one or the whole series".Error prevention

  7. 7

    Undo on the message after cancelling. A wrong tap costs nothing.Let users recover from mistakes

Flow 6 · Reception dashboard · desktop

Run the front desk

Rana, at reception, starts the day by posting the status and keeping the seat count right, then checks people in as they arrive. Same look and words as the renter app.

2clicks to check someone in
Click Fatima's booking, or Check-in+1 click
  1. 1

    If today's status isn't posted, a banner says so with the button to fix it. It clears once staff post a notice marked as the daily status.Make important actions obvious · feedback

  2. 2

    Large − and + buttons for the seat count, and a reminder that renters see this exact number.Fitts's Law · visibility of system status

  3. 3

    Every room's day on one timeline with a line for now. Booking state is shown by fill and outline, with a key.Visibility of status · labels, not colour alone

  4. 4

    Click a booking to check its renter in; click free time to book for someone with the form already filled.Fewer clicks for the most common desk tasks

  5. 5

    Today's bookings sort by time, room, renter or status, because the desk gets different questions.User control

  6. 6

    A new walk-in uses the same sign-up form as the app: name, phone, email.Consistency

  7. 7

    A booking only counts from 30 minutes before it starts. Someone arriving early is a walk-in until then, and the button says so.Prevent errors · match the real process

  8. 8

    The main button names exactly what will happen. When public seating is full, the walk-in button says "Public seating full" and is disabled.Feedback · prevent errors

Edge states · Renter app

When something goes wrong

Each of these screens tells the person what happened and gives a way forward. None of them is a dead end.

  1. 1

    Log in is asked for at the moment it's needed and explains why. After logging in, the person lands back on the booking they started.Context · don't lose the user's work

  2. 2

    No password: a 4-digit code by SMS. Sign up asks only name, phone and email; the rest can wait for the profile.Reduce friction · fewer fields

  3. 3

    The clash is explained in one sentence, followed by the nearest free times in the same room and other free rooms at the same time.Help users recover from errors

  4. 4

    A room that is down says why, and the only button leads to another room.Visibility of status · a way forward

  5. 5

    A pause names the reason, the rule and the date it ends, and leaves browsing open.Clear, fair feedback

Prevent errors, allow recoveryStop mistakes early; make them cheap to undoDisabled buttons with reasons, skipped days named before confirming, Undo, Slot just taken

The Aesthetic-Usability Effect belongs to the next stage. These frames are deliberately plain; the prototype adds the visual design.

Next step

Test the structure with real people

Every decision above comes from our own reviews. Before more polish, five people should try the flows: three renters and two reception staff, three tasks each.

Tasks: find a free room right now · book the same room on two days · cancel one booking. For reception: check in someone who arrives early.

Watch for: where they hesitate, how many taps they really need compared with the counts above, and what they expected a button to do. Change the wireframe first, then the prototype.
